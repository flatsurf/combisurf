oriented_map#

Combinatorial maps on oriented surfaces

The main class in this module is OrientedMap which describes a cell decomposition of an oriented surface.

A walk on a map is a word (see combisurf.word) whose letters are active half-edges of the map and such that each half-edge ends at the vertex where the next one starts. It can be given as a string such as "0,~1", a list of integers or an array('i'). A walk is closed if moreover its last half-edge ends at the vertex where its first one starts.

A multiwalk is a finite collection of walks with non-negative integer multiplicities. It can be given as a single walk (with multiplicity one), a list of walks [w0, w1, ...] (each with multiplicity one) or a list of pairs [(w0, m0), (w1, m1), ...]. It is taken as given: backtracking is not simplified, repeated walks are not merged and conjugate closed walks are not identified.

class combisurf.oriented_map.OrientedMap(vp=None, fp=None, mutable=False, check=True)[source]#

Map on oriented surface.

An OrientedMap is encoded by three permutations called the vertex permutation, the edge permutation and the face permutation. The edge permutation is always implicit and the vertex and face permutations are always abbreviated as vp and fp. The cycles in the cycle decomposition of vp and fp encode respectively the vertices and the faces of the map. The domain of the permutations is the set of half-edges of the map. Each half-edge could either be paired to make an edge, or be alone and form a folded edge.

EXAMPLES:

sage: from combisurf import OrientedMap

The most user friendly way to initialize an OrientedMap is to use strings that describe the cycle decomposition of the vertex and face permutations. Here, edges correspond to pair of elements of the form i and ~i. Here is a map made of three edges:

sage: OrientedMap(vp="(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", fp="(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")
OrientedMap("(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", "(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")

Since vertex and face permutations are redundant, you can provide only one of them:

sage: OrientedMap(vp="(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)")
OrientedMap("(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", "(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")
sage: OrientedMap(fp="(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")
OrientedMap("(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", "(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")

Under the hood, the vertex permutation of the above example turns out to be 0 mapsto 5, 1 mapsto 6, 2 mapsto 1, 3 mapsto 7, 4 mapsto 4, 5 mapsto 3, 6 mapsto 0 7 mapsto 2. Or in cycle notation (0,5,3,7,2,1,6)(4). More precisely, half-edges in the map use non-negative integers and an even half-edge is always paired to the half-edge with the next integer (so that i and ~i in the string notation correspond to 2i and 2i+1 in the actual permutation):

sage: m = OrientedMap(vp="(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", fp="(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")
sage: m.vertex_permutation()
array('i', [5, 6, 1, 7, 4, 3, 0, 2])
sage: from combisurf.permutation import perm_cycle_string
sage: perm_cycle_string(m.vertex_permutation())
'(0,5,3,7,2,1,6)(4)'

One can alternatively initialize an oriented map with a permutation either as a list of images:

sage: OrientedMap(vp=[5, 6, 1, 7, 4, 3, 0, 2])
OrientedMap("(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", "(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")

Or as a list of disjoint cycles (including fixed points):

sage: OrientedMap(vp=[[0,5,3,7,2,1,6], [4]])
OrientedMap("(0,~2,~1,~3,1,~0,3)(2)", "(0,1,~2,2)(~0,3,~1,~3)")

It is not necessary for edges to be consecutive integers. One can for example initialize a map on a torus with edges 2 and 5:

sage: OrientedMap(vp="(2,5,~2,~5)")
OrientedMap("(2,5,~2,~5)", "(2,5,~2,~5)")

The corresponding vertex and face permutation of the above examples are lists of length 16 where only the images of 4, 5, 10 and 11 are different from -1:

sage: OrientedMap(vp="(2,5,~2,~5)").vertex_permutation()
array('i', [-1, -1, -1, -1, 10, 11, -1, -1, -1, -1, 5, 4])

In cycle notation, if an half-edge is not mentioned then the corresponding edge is folded:

sage: OrientedMap(vp="(0,1)(~0)")
OrientedMap("(0,1)(~0)", "(0,~0,1)")
sage: OrientedMap(vp="(0,1)")
OrientedMap("(0,1)", "(0,1)")
sage: OrientedMap(vp=[[0,2],[1]])
OrientedMap("(0,1)(~0)", "(0,~0,1)")

Folded edge could also be initialized from list of images as follows:

sage: OrientedMap(vp=[2,1,0,-1])
OrientedMap("(0,1)(~0)", "(0,~0,1)")

Return generators of the automorphism group of this map with the given decorations.

The decorations and root_rule are given as in canonical_labelling(): the *_data matched exactly, the *_classes up to relabelling. The generators may differ from a root_rule to another, the group generated does not. The map may be disconnected (see automorphisms()); the generators then come from the structure of the group, not from the list of the automorphisms: without *_classes the group is a product of wreath products over the classes of isomorphic components, and with *_classes its subgroup preserving the partitions they define, searched by backtracking. The backtracking skips the placements of a component that are exchanged by an automorphism of its copy, so tied copies whose values are all mapped after the first components (k isolated edges with one or two class values) take a time polynomial in k; it stays exponential in the worst case, as on a graph on the class values given by its edges as isolated edges. This holds under every root_rule: on a disconnected map the generators do not depend on it, and the list of all the automorphisms (of length at least k! on k isomorphic components) is never computed.

With a key (a callable on relabellings, see canonical_labelling()), the group is the subgroup of automorphisms() with the same key, and the generators are taken from the list of its elements, which is computed in full: one call of key per automorphism of the whole group (at least k! on k isomorphic components). An element is kept when it is not in the group generated by those kept so far. The requirement on key is stated in canonical_labelling().

INPUT:

  • half_edge_data, edge_data, vertex_data, face_data, half_edge_classes, edge_classes, vertex_classes, face_classes, check, root_rule, key – as in canonical_labelling()

OUTPUT: a list of automorphisms (as in automorphisms()) that generate the group; empty when the group is trivial.

Return the automorphisms of this map with the given decorations.

The decorations and root_rule are given as in canonical_labelling(): the *_data matched exactly, the *_classes up to relabelling. The list is the same group under every root_rule, in the order of the search of the rule. On a disconnected map the automorphisms may permute isomorphic components, and a *_classes decoration is preserved up to one bijection of its values for all the components together. The list is computed in full under every root_rule: it has at least k! elements on a map with k isomorphic components, which automorphism_generators() avoids.

With a key (a callable on relabellings, see canonical_labelling()), the list is reduced to the automorphisms a with key(r ∘ a) == key(r), r the labelling of canonical_labelling() with the same key, in the same order: the stabiliser of what key encodes. One call of key is made per automorphism of the full list. The requirement on key is stated in canonical_labelling().

  • half_edge_data, edge_data, vertex_data, face_data, half_edge_classes, edge_classes, vertex_classes, face_classes, check, root_rule, key – as in canonical_labelling()

OUTPUT: the list of the automorphisms, the identity first, each an array('i') permutation of the half-edges that commutes with the face and vertex permutations, preserves the edges and the decorations (the *_classes up to a bijection of their values), and is the identity on the inactive edges. It is a relabelling accepted by relabel().

Return the canonical labelling of this map with the given decorations.

The *_data decorations are matched exactly, the *_classes decorations up to relabelling. A disconnected map is labelled component after component, and the values of a *_classes decoration are numbered across the components, so that its classes may tie several components together.

INPUT:

  • half_edge_data – optional; either one decoration, a list or array indexed by half-edges, or several decorations, given as a tuple whose entries are all lists, tuples or array.array; any other tuple, and any other value, is one decoration. The values are integers in the range of a C int

  • edge_data – optional; a list indexed by edges, of integers in the range of a C int

  • vertex_data, face_data – optional; lists indexed by half-edges, constant on each vertex and on each face respectively

  • half_edge_classes, edge_classes, vertex_classes, face_classes – optional; decorations of the same shapes as half_edge_data, edge_data, vertex_data and face_data, matched up to relabelling

  • check – boolean (default True); whether to check the lengths of the decorations, that their values are integers and the constancy of vertex_data, face_data, vertex_classes and face_classes

  • root_rule – string (default "half-edge-fewest+data": decorations narrow the roots tried, and on an undecorated map it is "half-edge-fewest", which tries fewer roots than "all": an exploration of the GL(2, Z)-orbit of an origami of 50 squares took 1.44 s instead of 2.81 s); the half-edges tried as roots of the canonical labelling, among those of one class of half-edges preserved by every isomorphism: "all" tries every half-edge; "half-edge-max" and "half-edge-fewest" group the half-edges by the degrees of their vertex and of their face; "edge-max" and "edge-fewest" by these degrees for the half-edge and for its twin, and whether the two are at the same vertex and in the same face. "*-max" tries the class of the largest invariant, "*-fewest" the class with the fewest half-edges (ties broken by the largest invariant); on a disconnected map the class is chosen among the half-edges not labelled yet, component after component. Each rule but "all" followed by "+data" ("half-edge-max+data", "half-edge-fewest+data", "edge-max+data", "edge-fewest+data") appends to the invariant of a half-edge the values of the *_data decorations at it (and at its twin under the edge rules), never those of the *_classes: the classes are finer, so fewer roots are tried on a decorated map, and without *_data the results are those of the rule without "+data". The labellings and the canonical forms under two rules are not comparable. Any other value raises a ValueError, whatever check. See combisurf.isomorphism_naive for the definitions.

  • key – optional; a callable that takes a relabelling of this map (an array('i') sending each half-edge to its new label, as returned here) and returns a value comparable with < and ==. The relabellings that reach the minimal canonical key are the r0 ∘ a (h goes to r0[a[h]]) for a in automorphisms(), r0 the labelling without key; with key the result is the first of them of smallest value of key. One call of key is made per automorphism, on the full list of automorphisms (at least k! on k isomorphic components). With check=True a key that is not callable raises a TypeError. The labels given to the inactive edges are not determined by the isomorphism class: a key that depends on them does not give the same value on isomorphic maps.

    Requirement: key(r) must depend only on the map, with its decorations, relabelled by r. The minimisers of smallest value of key are then a coset of the automorphisms that key keeps, and these form a group. The code does not check it.

The values of a decoration are integers (Python int, Sage Integer, or anything with an __index__ method); the values on inactive half-edges are ignored. With check=True any other value raises a ValueError. The values of a *_data decoration are stored as C int: a value outside the range of a C int raises an OverflowError, whatever check. The values of a *_classes decoration are renumbered first and may be any integers.

A decoration matched exactly must take the same value on a half-edge and on its image by an isomorphism. A decoration matched up to relabelling only needs its values to correspond through a bijection: an isomorphism r preserves the decoration c of the first map and c' of the second when there is a bijection s of values with c'[r[h]] == s(c[h]) for every active half-edge h. Only the partition of the half-edges (edges, vertices, faces) into classes of equal values matters; for instance, the cylinders of a cylinder diagram pair its faces, and the pairing is given by face_classes with one value per cylinder.

fold_corner(h, check=2)[source]#

Fold the half-edge h onto the next half-edge in its face.

This operation consists in merging the head of next_in_face(h) with the tail of h, which identifies h with ep(next_in_face(h)) and ep(h) with next_in_face(h). Unless the face of h is a monogon (see below), the resulting map has one edge less and the face of h loses two from its degree. By convention it is the edge of h that disappears.

That identification rule settles the two degenerate corners as well. First, when next_in_face(h) is ep(h), both identifications read h with h and nothing is glued: the edge is pruned (via a call to delete_edge()). Secondly, when next_in_face(h) is h itself, that is when the face of h is a monogon, h is identified with ep(h) and the edge becomes a folded edge. This is the one case in which the edge of h survives the fold.

Neither the edge of h nor that of next_in_face(h) may be folded on entry: a NotImplementedError is raised when one of them is. The edge of h may well become folded by the fold itself, through the monogon case above.

INPUT:

  • h – a half-edge, the one whose edge is folded away

  • check – integer (default: 2); the level of checks to perform. Level 1 checks that the map is mutable, that h is one of its half-edges and that no folded edge is involved; level 0 performs none of that and assumes the caller has done it. No check is specific to level 2 here, which elsewhere in this class guards the expensive ones, as in genus() and relabel().

fold_half_edge(h, check=2)[source]#

Fold the half-edge h onto its reverse.

The half-edge h is identified with ep(h) so that the edge becomes a folded edge, of which only 2 * (h // 2) stays active. The position of h disappears from its face, the surviving half-edge takes over the position of ep(h), and the two ends of the edge are merged.

The result depends on h and not only on its edge: folding h and folding ep(h) give different maps in general, which is why this operation takes a half-edge where contract_edge() and delete_edge() take an edge.

A ValueError is raised if the edge of h is already folded.

INPUT:

Return the quad system of this map for the given forest and coforest.

The quad system is obtained from the radial map by contracting the edges of forest and deleting the edges of coforest, each of which amounts to folding the two corners of the corresponding quadrilateral, see fold_corner(). When forest and coforest are spanning, the result is a quadrangulation of the same surface with two vertices of degree 4g, 4g edges and 2g quadrilateral faces.

INPUT:

  • forest, coforest – (default: None) the half-edges of the edges to contract and to delete, in the format returned by forest_coforest_decomposition(); entries equal to -1 are ignored. When both are None a decomposition is computed. Listing the same edge twice raises a ValueError.

  • relabel – boolean (default: False); whether to relabel the result on 0, 1, ..., 2 * ne - 1 so that it has no inactive half-edge. Folding never renumbers, so without this the labels of the radial map are kept and are sparse. The relabelling goes edge by edge and preserves the parity of each half-edge inside its edge, so that the bipartition of the vertices stays visible on the labels.

radial_map(), fold_corner(), forest_coforest_decomposition()

ALGORITHM:

The folds are performed on a copy of the radial map, in which the edge e of this map is a quadrilateral face whose four sides alternate parity, the parity of a half-edge of the radial map being the side of the bipartition its tail belongs to. Contracting e folds the two even sides of that quadrilateral and deleting e folds the two odd ones.

Folding relabels: fold_corner() at a reads b = fp[a] and makes b take over the position of a ^ 1. So the sides computed on the initial radial map may be dead by the time they are needed. To avoid resolving names on the fly we keep, for each edge e, one live half-edge handle[e] lying on its quadrilateral together with the inverse map owner. The four sides are then recovered in constant time by walking the face of the handle, and the two of the wanted parity can both be read before folding, since two half-edges of equal parity are never opposite to each other. A handle dies only as the a or the a ^ 1 of a fold: in the first case its quadrilateral is the one being collapsed, that is the edge currently processed, and in the second case b is its replacement.

The projection, on the other hand, is only read once every fold has been performed. The dying half-edges are linked to the ones they are identified with and a single pass with path compression resolves the whole forest of links at the end, so that the method is linear.

radial_map(mapping=False, mutable=False)[source]#

Return the radial map of this map.

The radial map of an oriented map is the bipartite quadrangulation obtained by adding a vertex in the center of each face, joining this added vertex to every corner in the face and removing the original edges. The vertices of the radial map are in bijection with the union of vertices and faces of the original map. It has as many quadrilateral faces as edges in the original map.

The convention used for labelling is that the half-edge of the radial map to the left of h in the original map is labelled 2h. That way, vertices of the radial map are either cycles of positively oriented edges or cycles of negatively oriented edges. In particular, the bipartition of vertices is visible on the labelling.

tree_cotree_decomposition(root_vertex=0, root_face=0)[source]#

Return a tree cotree decomposition as a triple (tree, cotree, complementary_edges).

INPUT:

  • root_vertex – (default: 0) the vertex the tree is rooted at

  • root_face – (default: 0) the face the cotree is rooted at

OUTPUT: a triple (tree, cotree, complementary_edges) of arrays of integers. The tree and cotree have length respectively the number of vertices and the number of faces in the map. We describe tree below and the cotree is similar.

  • tree[root_vertex] is -1

  • for a non-root vertex v, tree[v] is a half-edge adjacent to v and going out from the root.

The last entry complementary_edges is the array of edge indices that are neither part of the tree nor the cotree.

EXAMPLES:

sage: from combisurf import OrientedMap
sage: m = OrientedMap(vp="(2,3,1,5,6,~5)(~1,4,~2,~6)(~3,~4)")
sage: tree, cotree, comp_edges = m.tree_cotree_decomposition()
sage: tree
array('i', [-1, 3, 7])
sage: cotree
array('i', [-1, 9, 4])
sage: comp_edges
array('i', [5, 6])

To obtain the edges used in the tree and cotree respectively, one can do it as follows (and check that we indeed obtain a partition of edges):

sage: tree_edges = [h // 2 for h in tree if h != -1]
sage: tree_edges
[1, 3]
sage: cotree_edges = [h // 2 for h in cotree if h != -1]
sage: cotree_edges
[4, 2]
