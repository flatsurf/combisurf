Algorithms of the isomorphism code#

The algorithms of the functions of omap.h: oriented maps in C. The definitions (maps and their decorations, relabelling from a root, keys, rules of the roots, blocks) and the contracts of the functions (arguments, buffers and their sizes, return values) are in omap.h: oriented maps in C. Below, n is the number of half-edges, nd the number of data arrays, nc the number of class arrays, and \(|\mathrm{Aut}|\) the number of automorphisms of the decorated map.

Invariants of two maps#

domap_invariants_differ() compares, in this order, stopping at the first difference:

  • for each data array, a hash of the multiset of its values on the active half-edges (one pass, \(O(n)\));

  • then for each class array, the multiset of the sizes of its classes (counted, \(O(n)\)).

The multisets of the face degrees and of the vertex degrees are not compared: walking the cycles of the two maps is one more pass over each map, on top of those the search makes anyway, and it rejects few pairs that the other invariants accept.

Canonical key of a connected map#

domap_canonical() tries the roots of the class of the rule in increasing order and keeps the minimal key.

Rejection of the roots#

The first root is walked and keyed in full. Every other root is walked with its key compared with the best one as the entries of its face permutation part become final (those below the label of the next half-edge to walk), in passes of doubling length: the root is rejected at the first entry larger than the one of the best key, which costs about twice the labels it needed to differ, not n. At the first entry smaller, it becomes the best root and is walked and keyed in full. When the face permutation parts are equal, the data then the class parts are compared as they are built, with the same rejection. A root that ties with or beats the best one costs \(O(n (1 + nd + nc))\).

Orbits of the automorphisms found#

A root that ties with the best one gives an automorphism of the decorated map, which sends the best root to it; the orbits of the group generated by the automorphisms found are kept in a union-find over the half-edges (\(O(n)\) to initialise), represented by their smallest half-edge. Every half-edge of an orbit has the same key, so a root whose representative is smaller than itself has the key of an earlier root and is skipped at the cost of one find: either that key was rejected, or the root is in the orbit of the best root.

A tie is thus only met outside the orbit of the best root and strictly enlarges the group found: there are at most \(\log_2 |\mathrm{Aut}|\) of them, each costing \(O(n (1 + nd + nc))\) for the key and the automorphism. A map whose roots all tie (a regular map) costs \(O(n \log_2 |\mathrm{Aut}| (1 + nd + nc))\) for them instead of \(O(n^2 (1 + nd + nc))\). The roots of minimal key are the orbit of the best root, written by a final scan.

Class of the roots#

Under a rule other than DOMAP_ISO_ROOTS_ALL, the class costs \(O(n)\) more. The entries of the invariant, shifted by their minimum, are packed into one int in mixed radix over their ranges; when the product of the ranges is at most 2 n, the half-edges are counted per packed value and the class is collected by one scan, in \(O(n (1 + nd))\). Otherwise the half-edges are sorted by invariant by counting sorts on its 2 or 6 entries.

Under DOMAP_ISO_ROOTS_DATA each data array is then first replaced by order-preserving numbers of \(0, \ldots, n - 1\): the values minus their minimum when they span fewer than n values (\(O(n)\)), else their ranks among the active half-edges (a heap sort, \(O(n \log n)\) per array). They add nd or 2 nd entries.

Search over the components#

domap_iso_search() searches the lexicographically minimal sequence of blocks (Maps that may be disconnected) over the labellings of the map component after component.

Blocks and their order#

From a state (the components labelled so far), the class of the rule is chosen among the active half-edges not labelled yet (by the invariants of the whole map, and by the numbers of half-edges not labelled yet in each class under *_FEWEST). The half-edges of that class not labelled yet are tried as roots (every active half-edge not labelled yet under DOMAP_ISO_ROOTS_ALL), in increasing order, and only the roots of minimal block are followed, depth first in increasing root order; the component gets the labels following those used. A state whose sequence exceeds the prefix of the best sequence found is cut.

Extensions of tied roots#

When several roots tie, every one of them is followed if all_leaves is nonzero; otherwise only the first one if nc == 0, and the first one of each extension of the numbering of the class values if nc > 0. The extension of a tied root is, per class array, the values its component numbers first, in the order of their numbers.

Two tied roots of equal extensions put the same values at the same local labels, so that exchanging their components (or relabelling the component from one root to the other when it is the same one), the identity elsewhere, is an automorphism fixing the components labelled before; the subtree of the later root is the image of the one of the earlier root, searched first. A later tied root is compared with the earlier ones of equal block by three walks of their components, and the first match stops the comparison; when the block of the level numbers no new value, every extension is empty and no further tied root is tried.

Backjumps#

When all_leaves is 0, a leaf equal to the best one gives a backjump: the map from the best leaf to it is an automorphism (of the classes up to a relabelling of their values) fixing the components of the levels where the two leaves have the same roots, so the subtree of its root at the first level where they differ is the image of the one of the best leaf, already searched, and the search returns at once to that level and its next tied root.

Cost#

With the cut of equal extensions, k tied copies of a component cost one path per distinct extension at each level: one path for k isolated edges with one class value, whose automorphisms are \(2^k k!\), and two for \(b + 1\) edges with the value 0 beside b with the value 1. Tied roots of different extensions are all followed, and their subtrees may part only deep, so that the search stays exponential in the worst case, as for a graph on the class values given by its edges as isolated edges.

With all_leaves nonzero neither of these cuts applies: every leaf reaching the minimum is reported, as many as the automorphisms.

Stabiliser of the classes#

domap_stabiliser() computes the group \(H\) of the automorphisms of the map, laid out by a domap_wreath_struct, that preserve the partitions defined by its class arrays.

The group \(G\) of the automorphisms of the map with its data alone is the product, over the kinds of k isomorphic components \(C\), of the wreath products \(\mathrm{Aut}(C) \wr S_k\), each of order at least \(k!\). Without class arrays \(H = G\), and the Cython module reads its generators and its order from the kinds, without a search and without listing the elements of the group (automorphism_generators(), canonical_key()).

Subgroup search#

\(H\) is the stabiliser of these partitions in \(G\), computed by a subgroup search of Sims. In this search, the base is the sequence \(b_0, \ldots, b_{ncomp-1}\) of the base points, one per level: the half-edge of canonical label 0 of each level (lab[off[i]]); only the identity of \(G\) fixes them all. The pointwise stabiliser of \(b_0, \ldots, b_{i-1}\) in \(G\) fixes their components and permutes the others as the wreath products do, so the orbit of \(b_i\) in it is made of the half-edges whose label is one of labels[loff[t]], ..., labels[loff[t + 1] - 1] in the copies of the kind t of \(b_i\) at the levels \(\geq i\).

The levels are processed from the last to the first: each point \(x\) of that orbit outside the orbit of \(b_i\) under the generators found so far (a union-find over the half-edges) is tried by a depth-first search for an element of \(H\), the identity on the levels \(< i\), sending \(b_i\) to \(x\), which maps the following levels one after the other to unused copies of their kinds and rejects a step whose values contradict the bijections of values built so far. The first element found is a generator.

Orbit sizes#

The generators found at the levels \(\geq i\) generate the stabiliser of \(b_0, \ldots, b_{i-1}\) in \(H\), so the order of \(H\) is the product over the levels of the size of the orbit of \(b_i\) right after its level. A level that is the only copy of its kind and has a single root label has an orbit of size 1 and is skipped.

Skipped placements#

Two placements of a level adding no entry to the bijections of values, onto the copies \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) (or twice onto one copy), put equal values at the labels they match: the automorphism of the copy relating their labels is an isomorphism from \(c_1\) to \(c_2\) carrying the values, and exchanging \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) through it, the identity on the levels placed before, is an element of \(H\) that sends a completion of either placement onto one of the other. After one of them failed, the others are skipped, at the levels of the depth-first search and among the candidates \(x\), which are then outside the orbit of \(b_i\) exactly when the earlier one is.

Cost#

On tied copies whose values are all mapped after the first levels, such as k isolated edges with one or two class values in any pattern, each level then descends into at most one failed subtree per entry, and the search takes a time polynomial in the number of components. It stays exponential in the worst case, when the placements map new values and the values of classes reject them only deep in the search, as on a graph on the class values given by its edges as isolated edges (the two values of an edge being its endpoints): the search is then one for the automorphisms of the graph.
